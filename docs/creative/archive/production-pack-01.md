Working creative specification · 6 September 2026

## Episode 01: Options

A 15-second talking-cat short. Three cats insist that a door be opened and then refuse to go through it. The payoff is MO's solemn explanation: “We requested options.”

The storyboard's sixth frame labels the return to the opening action. In the final timeline the repeated demand occurs when playback loops, not as an additional line squeezed into the last second. The generated storyboard is a framing reference: clean up the doubled door hardware in the open-door panels and build the first/last frames from one master background for an exact loop. Preserve the open-door state behind MO in the close-up.

Voice direction: MEO speaks with misplaced urgency; ME asks a practical question; MO sounds like a calm official reading a policy. Use original performers or licensed synthetic voices. No celebrity impersonation. Record three takes per line: dry, quieter, and slightly faster. Choose timing by listening to the whole exchange.

Sound brief: original three-note vocal phrase, approximately one second in total, softly descending on the final note. Record clean dry stems and a mixed version. Keep peaks controlled and every word intelligible on phone speakers. No third-party meme audio needed.

Delivery target: 1080 × 1920, 30 fps, 15 seconds. Leave generous room for platform interface overlays and inspect previews on each destination. Keep captions near the middle-lower area without covering faces. Use at most two short lines, ivory text with a dark outline. Supply clean and captioned versions. Attached SRT is a timing draft for these exact lines; adjust after voice recording.

Asset checklist: three consistent character masters; closed/open door background; door layer with hinge pivot; foreground masks; three dialogue recordings; room tone; latch/hinge effect; original vocal signature; captions. Character sheet is concept reference and needs artist cleanup before becoming a production model or rig.

## Character consistency

MEO: tangerine, lifted chin, confident outward gaze. ME: ivory, level head, economical eye movement. MO: charcoal, slightly misaligned gaze, delayed response. Keep height differences subtle and mouths tiny. All retain the cobalt forehead square.

Create six mouth states per character: rest, small open, wide small oval, rounded O, closed M/B/P, soft corner change. They remain graphic marks; never add human lips. Limit body animation to a subtle pivot or squash. Preserve foot/body contact so figures do not drift.

Approve front, three-quarter, side, and speaking reference views before generating further episodes. Compare forehead marks, eye spacing, ear height, material, and silhouette against the reference every time. The generated studies do not prove consistent hidden 3D geometry.

## First release batch

Publish Options, Bowl, Braincell, Mirror, Sunbeam, Keyboard, and Water as the first seven distinct episodes, at a sustainable pace. They cover social contradiction, cat logic, recurring lore, and wordplay. Use the remaining scripts as the next batch and rewrite them using observed viewer responses.

For Options, test “Open the door. It's urgent.” against “The door is violating my rights.” in a later comparable episode. Do not treat differences between separate posts as causal proof: distribution and timing also differ.

Prepare reaction exports: “We requested options”; “Presence”; “International waters”; “Peer reviewed”; “Signal lost”; “The future called”; “Decline”; “I prefer live events.” Each gets a face crop and a short silent loop. One reaction must remain understandable when separated from the original scene.

Use the experiment-log.csv to record results after a consistent observation period, such as 48 hours and seven days. Blank values mean unmeasured. Count shares and follows separately for each platform; do not merge raw views across platforms as though their definitions match.

## Collectible study: 24 portraits before a mint

Create eight states for each of the three cats: neutral, urgent, unimpressed, buffering, witnessed something, tiny triumph, pretending to understand, and asleep on duty. Keep these as an internal study initially. Avoid rarity percentages until edition size and final artwork are defined.

Hold camera, crop, background families, lighting, and forehead mark constant. Change pose and eyes first. Add a single episode-linked prop only when it improves the story, such as a door key for Options or one keyboard key for Keyboard. Collectible titles can cite the episode so the artwork records a moment fans recognize.

Judge every portrait without a price or rarity label: is it readable at avatar size, distinctive within its row, attractive at full size, and useful as a reaction? Review the whole grid for repetitive or accidental near-duplicates. Select a smaller finished set if quality varies.

The commercial promise is authorship, consistent execution, and a character people care about. Price, demand, exchange support, and rights require separate decisions. The original brief's Robinhood-program question remains unresolved; nothing in this pack assumes stock rewards or commits to a 404 contract.
